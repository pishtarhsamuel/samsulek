Base fields: mintAuthority and freezeAuthority must be null. For pump.fun no launchpad exception is needed: create_v2 revokes the mint authority inside the creation transaction and never sets a freeze authority (rows 15-16); legacy pump tokens look the same (row 21). ## Measurements

- RPC budget: 34 of 150 calls, all read-only. 9 refused with 429: #7 getTokenLargestAccounts (method limit 0); getSignaturesForAddress #17, #19, #30, #31, #32 and getTransaction #22, #25, #26 (method limit 10 exhausted on the team's shared IP; remaining went to -12). One -32015 (#2, tx version). No 403s from the RPC.
- Static checks: 1 getMultipleAccounts (base64, up to 100 mints) per batch, parsed locally; +1 call for curve/pool accounts.
- Holders: 1 getProgramAccounts per Token-2022 mint (memcmp mint + dataSlice 40 bytes); size grows with holder count; 10 per window on the public RPC.
- Bundle check on one active coin, ~6 min after launch: 3 getSignaturesForAddress pages (200 + 1,000 + 318) to reach the creation slot, then 1 getTransaction per early tx (7 here), >= 2 per buyer for funding (often more), >= 1 for the creator: roughly 15-30 calls per token. Public limits (10 per ~10 s per IP for both methods) allow at most a few tokens per minute for the whole team; on Helius free (1 credit each, 10 req/s) ~30 credits per token, so 1M credits ~ 33,000 checks per month.
- Simulation: rows 31-32 (no signature needed; a missing fee payer gives AccountNotFound).
- Slot time ~0.213 s (row 36); getMultipleAccounts latency not tracked.

## What this means for the bot

1. Static checks are cheap and reliable, but they catch little on pump.fun: every pump.fun coin read (fresh create_v2, mayhem, legacy) had no mint/freeze authority and only metadata extensions with null authorities. They pass by construction. LIBRA passed the same checks and still rugged by pulling liquidity. "Passes the static check" must never mean "safe".
2. Read the mint as base64 and parse the TLV ourselves with an allowlist of type numbers and lengths; reject unknown types. Use jsonParsed only as a cross-check: it can silently drop every extension (row 13).
3. Holder concentration: getTokenLargestAccounts is switched off on the public RPC; the Token-2022 memcmp getProgramAccounts works (1 call). Exclude the bonding-curve ATA, the Mayhem vault, PumpSwap pool vaults and burn addresses by owner; an owner that is off the ed25519 curve is a PDA (program vault), checkable offline. In mayhem coins half the supply sits in a vault that trades: exclude it from concentration but treat it as an active seller.
4. Sellability: simulation needs no signature, only an existing fee payer with enough SOL. So the bot can, read-only, simulate one transaction [create ATA, buy, sell all] with its own public key as payer and compare pre/post SOL and token balances (round-trip loss), or simulate a sell from a current holder's wallet. This catches sell-blocking hooks, pauses, frozen-by-default accounts, non-transferable tokens and fees as of that moment. It does NOT catch a later freeze, pause, hook install, fee increase (26-51 h notice) or a permanent-delegate burn, and it runs on a recent state, not the block we land in. Re-check before every sell. The bot wallet must hold enough SOL for the simulated buy (the owner funds it by hand). A quote (Jupiter) only proves a route exists on paper (its docs were blocked: UNVERIFIED).
5. Bundles and creator history are visible on-chain (same-slot buys, identical amounts, fresh wallets), but tracing funding costs many calls and hit public limits within minutes. Take first-slot buyers and creators from our own creation recorder as they happen and keep our own creator -> mints history; do not crawl wallet histories per token on the public RPC.
6. Parser hygiene: transaction version 1 is live; always pass maxSupportedTransactionVersion 1 and fail closed on unknown versions. Use blockTime, not slot counts, for token age (slots are ~0.21 s now, not 0.4 s).
7. Third-party scanners were not verified (blocked). If used later: one extra vote, never the only check, and "scanner down" means "do not buy".
8. Honest bottom line: these filters reduce the chance of being trapped, but with $3-5 trades in pump.fun coins most losses will come from ordinary dumps, dev and bundle selling, fees and slippage, not exotic extensions. A filter that passes most pump.fun coins does not create an edge.
9. Brief check: the brief's launchpad exception for mint/freeze authority is not needed for pump.fun (authorities are already null during the curve); other launchpads are unchecked.
